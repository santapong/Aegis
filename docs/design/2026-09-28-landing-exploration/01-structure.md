# Aegis landing: structure before visual design

Date: 2026-09-28. Status: exploration in response to the owner's request for more ideas and broader website research. The existing `/landing` is a working first draft, not an approved final direction. This exploration does not replace it.

## Positioning

Primary visitor: someone who wants to organize day-to-day personal finances and understand how upcoming bills, income, savings, and plans fit together. Use familiar life situations before technical product terms. Self-hosting and open source are trust/ownership differentiators, not the first thing an ordinary visitor must understand.

Aegis's strongest distinctive story is **money arranged in time**: calendar events and Gantt plans connect today's transactions to future goals. The current page communicates general clarity well, but its dashboard-first hero and equal feature cards could belong to many finance products. Develop a narrative around a concrete month and the visitor's next decision.

Primary action: create an account at `/register`. Secondary action: explore a clearly labeled sample month. Returning user action: `/login`. Documentation and source remain easy to find.

## Content architecture — draft before reference research

| Order | Visitor's question | Section purpose | Evidence / interaction | Desired next step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Header | Where am I, and can I return to my account? | Aegis identity; three useful destinations | Product / How it works / Your data; sign-in; one primary CTA | Orient or sign in |
| 2. Hero + immediate proof | What does Aegis help me do? | Explain planning money over time in one sentence | A focused week/month: income, rent, a savings contribution. Show this with the message, not after a full empty screen | Explore sample or create account |
| 3. A month in focus | How would this help my life? | Follow one coherent scenario from money in to plans | Select a real sample event; see amount, date, and purpose. Switch Calendar / Timeline rather than unrelated feature tabs | Understand the distinctive workflow |
| 4. From a record to a plan | How much work is this? | Explain the actual three-step workflow | Record income/expenses → arrange upcoming plans → review budgets/goals. Use product crops and short copy | Believe it is understandable |
| 5. Room for real life | Is this useful for my situation? | Show 2–3 different applications of the same system | Emergency fund, trip planning, debt payoff; connect each to a relevant view. Avoid an undifferentiated feature inventory | Recognize their own use case |
| 6. Insights with context | What does AI add? | Explain optional assistance after the core utility is clear | One labeled example response using the same sample amounts; state provider setup and external-provider data handling | Understand value and limits |
| 7. Ownership and practical trust | What happens to my data? | Explain real control and setup | MIT source, documented self-hosting, export capability. Distinguish self-hosting from an absolute privacy claim | Read docs/source or continue |
| 8. Questions + close | What would stop me from starting? | Resolve bank-sync, setup, AI, and hosting questions | Native FAQ plus a clear return to registration; no invented trial, price, reviews, or customer logos | Start with Aegis |

This architecture is a hierarchy of questions, not eight mandatory full-screen bands. Combine workflow and use cases where concise, and combine ownership with FAQ where needed. Keep the first useful example visible in the first viewport or immediately entering it.

## Content boundaries

- Confirmed locally from the project: transaction recording, budgets, calendar plans, Gantt view, savings, debts, trips, provider-configured AI, exports and documented self-hosting.
- Do not imply bank synchronization, autonomous transfers, guaranteed financial results, live merchant coverage, security certification, active customer adoption, or a confirmed free commercial plan.
- The ongoing LINE integration has local work but is not a public launch claim.
- Example amounts must be labeled and internally consistent. A concept visualization is not a real account screenshot.
- Preserve Aegis's name and existing icon. An exploration may propose a treatment, but does not approve a replacement logo.

## Questions for the visual exploration

1. Can Aegis's existing amber/teal identity make the product more recognizable?
2. Does a DravenIQ navy/silver/cyan treatment help explain temporal planning, rather than simply recolor a generic dashboard?
3. Would a light editorial body make personal finance feel more approachable while retaining a dark Aegis brand zone?
4. Which direction connects hero, product evidence, and next scroll most naturally?

Next artifact: a reference matrix based on current pages, then three distinct annotated wireframes. Choose the narrative and visual direction before another application rewrite.
